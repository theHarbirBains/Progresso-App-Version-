# CLAUDE.md — Progresso Development Rules

This file contains the **permanent** working rules for any coding agent (Claude Code or otherwise) operating in this repository. Product/domain detail lives in [docs/PROGRESSO_MASTER_SPEC.md](docs/PROGRESSO_MASTER_SPEC.md) — read both before doing substantial work.

## Current Phase

**Actively developed, past Foundation.** The core loop is implemented and working end to end: accounts/onboarding, workout splits (create/edit/view/duplicate/delete + presets), live workout tracking, workout history, and Progress/PR/1RM analytics, plus a working (if simpler) nutrition-logging feature and an app-wide Settings hub. See [README.md](README.md) for the current feature list and [docs/PROGRESSO_MASTER_SPEC.md](docs/PROGRESSO_MASTER_SPEC.md) for the roadmap.

Reminders (explicitly requested and implemented, iPhone only -- `apps/mobile/src/notifications`) are real: local notifications scheduled on the phone with expo-notifications, never sent from a server. Settings > Notifications turns each on or off and sets its time: Training Nudges (no days or time to set: from the second day without a workout, every other day at 5:00 PM, what's up next in their split or the muscle they've gone longest without -- a week or more -- taking turns, `reminderPlan.ts`'s `trainingNudge`; a workout finished moves them all on), PR Cheers ("KEEP GOING, HARBIR!" as a workout is finished with a new PR, from the Share screen it lands on), Weekly Summary (Sundays, at 12:00 AM unless changed), and, only with Nutrition in the membership, Log Your Meals (if nothing's logged that day) and the Protein & Calorie Check-in (the day's figures). All are on by default for someone who said yes to notifications (`pushNotificationsOptIn`) on a phone that allows them, and nothing is scheduled otherwise or once signed out. The settings are kept on the phone, per person (`reminderSettings.ts`). A scheduled notification can't look anything up when it fires, so `RemindersProvider` makes the next 7 days' plan (`reminderPlan.ts`) again from the shared caches whenever they change -- a workout finished drops today's workout reminder, a food logged drops today's meal reminder and redoes the check-in -- and on coming back to the front. A tapped reminder opens its screen through the app's links (`navigation/linking.ts`). Server-sent push is still the incomplete part below.

Some areas remain intentionally incomplete rather than hidden — do not treat these as done: server-sent push notifications (devices register their tokens; social pushes -- a follow request or accept, a like, a comment -- are sent through Expo the moment they happen (`notifications/social-push.service.ts`, each behind its own switch, `users.notify_*`), but the API's daily reminder job has nothing triggering it -- there is no scheduler and its `INTERNAL_NOTIFICATIONS_SECRET` is unset -- and iOS delivery of any server push also needs an APNs key in EAS; the only email is the welcome email -- `apps/api/src/users/welcome-email.service.ts`, sent once through Resend's REST API as onboarding is first completed, an account email that doesn't wait on `email_opt_in`, and nothing at all until `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM` are set), Change Email (visible but "Coming Soon"), and the admin surface beyond the Feedback Inbox (authorization is real and tested; there is no admin dashboard -- the in-app Feedback Inbox is the one admin page so far). Do not implement these, AI features, or anything else beyond the current feature set until explicitly approved.

Apple Health and the home-screen widgets (explicitly requested and implemented, iPhone only) are real too, and deliberately narrow. Apple Health is **read steps only** (`src/health/steps.ts`): today's count, read on the phone each time Feed shows it, and each day's count for the last four weeks on Progress's Steps tab, read each time it opens -- never stored in Supabase or sent to the API, nothing written to Health, no background delivery -- reading or writing anything else needs approval, and so does storing steps anywhere (it would change the Privacy Policy, account export and deletion). The widgets (`src/widgets`, `expo-widgets`) are Today's Nutrition, Next Workout and Today's Steps on the Home Screen, plus the Lock Screen workout card (a Live Activity) while a workout or group workout is going. A widget runs outside the app and can't fetch: the app hands it what it already has, and every widget is cleared on sign-out. Each is handed a week of days at a time, so the date at its top turns over at midnight by itself. They wear the app's theme (its accent and photo, `widgets/widgetLook.ts`): a widget can only read a file in the App Group, so the app copies the theme's widget photos there (`widgets/widgetPhotos.ts`, from `assets/widgets`) -- **a widget's image must be no bigger, in pixels, than the smallest widget it fills**, or WidgetKit draws the widget blank. Both native libraries are loaded on first use (`health/steps.ts`, `widgets/loadWidgets.ts`), never imported where the app starts. **Metro treats a file that fails while it loads after startup as fatal, even inside a try** (`guardedLoadModule` reports it with `reportFatalError`), so a lazily loaded file must do nothing native as it loads: the widgets are created in `homeWidgets()`, a plain call `loadWidgets` catches a failure from (shown in a one-time alert); Apple Health's errors show on the Steps card and the Steps tab. **Platform files must share their fallback's extension** (`homeWidgets.ios.tsx` / `homeWidgets.tsx`): Metro tries every platform for `.ts` before any `.tsx`, so a `homeWidgets.ts` would be picked over `homeWidgets.ios.tsx` on iPhone -- Jest resolves the other way, so tests can't see it; builds 28 to 30 shipped the do-nothing fallback this way and crashed at launch, and `src/platformFiles.test.ts` now fails on it. **A widget's props must never hold a null** (or undefined, or a non-finite number): expo-widgets keeps them in iOS's UserDefaults, a null arrives there as NSNull, and UserDefaults refuses it by crashing the whole app -- builds 28 and 29 crashed at launch this way. Leave an unknown value out instead (`widgets/widgetProps.ts`); everything handed to a widget goes through `plistSafe`, and Jest's expo-widgets stand-in fails any test that hands a widget something the phone couldn't store. The Privacy Policy's Apple Health and Widgets paragraphs describe exactly this; change them with it.

Onboarding's **Find your country** (explicitly requested -- `onboarding/countryFromLocation.ts`, expo-location) is real and deliberately narrow: where the build has it, a step before the country list asks for the phone's location once, "while using the app" at reduced accuracy (app.json -- no Always, background or motion permission), and only the country iPhone's geocoder names comes back, to list it first (`draft.locatedCountry`, never saved to the account -- only the country picked is). Nothing else asks for location; using it for anything more needs approval. The Privacy Policy's "Your location, while you sign up" paragraph says exactly this; change them together. Onboarding asks **What is your sex?** -- Man or Woman only, what the calorie formula and nutrient tables work from (a profile saved earlier with another answer keeps it) -- and offers six fitness goals (General Health and Other went; saved ones stay on profiles).

Achievement Points and lifetime ranks (explicitly requested and implemented -- `supabase/migrations/20261118100000_achievement_levels.sql`, `apps/mobile/src/achievements`) are real too: 38 achievements in `achievement_definitions`, 11 single-unlock and 27 multi-level, with every level's goal and AP in `achievement_levels` (203 levels, 58,230 AP in all -- each level's goal about 2-4x the last and its AP about 1.5-2x, so higher levels pay more but cost more per AP). Twelve are muscle-group volume (Pecs Appeal to Iron Core): each finished weighted set counts once, toward its exercise's own muscle group -- Progresso has no secondary muscles -- and core counts finished sets of any kind. Six are cardio (explicitly requested -- `20261209100000_cardio_achievements.sql`, a Cardio category): Heart Starter, Hybrid Athlete (lifting and cardio in one workout) and Cross Trainer (five kinds) single, and Engine Room (workouts with cardio), Long Haul (minutes of cardio) and Mile Marker (distance, set in miles and stored in km -- the `km` unit, shown in mi or km by the person's weight unit) with levels; only valid workouts' cardio counts, each activity at most 4 hours and 100 km toward them. Consistency is weeks trained in all, never in a row; there are no weekly or monthly repeatable achievements and nothing tied to the weekly goal (removed at Harbir's request: a missed week made up later loses nothing). Only the server awards (`award_achievements` -- through `award_my_achievements` as the person signs in, comes back to the front, opens Achievements, finishes onboarding, and a moment after the shared workout, record or food caches change; and for everyone, whether or not their app has asked: `award_everyone` nightly at 04:45 UTC (pg_cron), the API before it shows anyone's profile, and the API as a tracked history is claimed -- never for an unclaimed placeholder, whose history counts once claimed): it works every figure out from the real tables (reusing `person_profile_totals`), in the person's own time zone (`users.time_zone`), one person at a time, inserting `achievement_awards` rows keyed (user, achievement, level) so no level is ever awarded twice, and keeps `achievement_progress` (each figure and level, never below one awarded). An award made while the person was away still gets its unlock: the app shows every award newer than `users.achievements_seen_at` and moves it on (`mark_achievements_seen`) once shown. **Achievements follow what's logged** (Harbir's call, `20261210100000_achievements_follow_what_is_logged.sql`, replacing "never taken back"): every award run takes back a level whose figure no longer reaches its goal -- a workout deleted, a set or cardio activity corrected, a split or saved meal removed -- its AP with it, so lifetime AP and the rank from it can go down; social achievements (friends, likes -- other people's doing) stay once earned, and nutrition ones aren't worked out (or taken back) without Nutrition in the membership. An award is otherwise never edited, and only the server makes or takes one back. Lifetime AP is their sum, and the rank comes from it (`achievements/ranks.ts`: Bronze 0, Silver 750, Gold 2,000, Platinum 4,000, Diamond 7,000, Vanguard 11,000, Titan 16,000, Sovereign 22,000, Legacy 30,000, five divisions each, Legacy levels every 2,000 past Legacy I; no decay, no seasons). A valid workout is finished, not deleted, with a finished set; friends are mutual follows; a received like counts only from someone who trains, at most 10 each. `audit_achievements` lists each level awarded, whether it's still reached and the running total, for support. Nutrition achievements are only ever their owner's and are only awarded with Nutrition in the membership (`award_achievements` skips them for Workouts; levels already awarded stay, and the Achievements page says so) -- a profile lists earned public ones, at their level, to accepted followers -- and profiles show only what's been earned. Challenges, body tracking (measurements, bodyweight log, progress photos), secondary muscles, exercise-specific strength milestones and seasonal leaderboards are left out and need approval. A new achievement or level is new rows in a migration; `npm run db:simulate` runs eight kinds of user (one cardio-focused; most do some cardio) through the real award functions for five years -- rebalance against it.

Feedback & Suggestions (explicitly requested and implemented -- `supabase/migrations/20261026100000_feedback_board.sql`, `apps/api/src/feedback`, `apps/mobile/src/feedback`) is real too: Settings > Support & About > Feedback & Suggestions sends a bug report, feature request, usability issue or general feedback (title, description, an optional screenshot, and the app version and platform it was sent from) through the API, which limits it to 5 an hour and 20 a day; the person reads their own, and its status history, straight from Supabase under RLS, and nothing else -- never an admin's notes. Support and full admins (RolesGuard, against `admin_users`) work the in-app Feedback Inbox (Settings > Admin, offered only to them): filter, search, change a status (New, Reviewing, Planned, In Progress, Completed, Declined) and add internal notes; every change and note is recorded in the append-only `admin_actions` (never a note's text). Screenshots are in a private bucket, each sender's in their own folder; deleting an account deletes the person's feedback, its history, its notes and their screenshots. Nothing sent can be edited or withdrawn. Public voting, public comments and any community-wide view of feedback still need approval.

Social v1 (explicitly requested and implemented — a one-directional, accept-gated follow graph plus a read-only Friends feed, with blocking and reporting; see `apps/api/src/follows`, `apps/api/src/feed`, `apps/api/src/safety`, and `apps/mobile/src/screens/FindPeopleScreen.tsx`) is real, backed functionality, not a placeholder. So is the rest of social, explicitly requested (`apps/api/src/social`, `20261114110000_social_profiles_likes_comments.sql`): a person's profile (`PersonProfileScreen` -- the You tab shows your own on the same page, `PersonProfilePage`, with no buttons on it: Edit Profile and Share Profile are behind its header's "..." (`social/OwnProfileSheet.tsx`, Harbir's call) -- who they are, their bio (`users.bio`), their follower/following counts and the mutual friends for anyone; for themselves or someone whose follow they accepted, their workout figures (workouts, last 30 days, records held, total volume, busiest month -- `person_profile_totals`), their lifetime rank (below) and the achievements they've earned, every exercise's best set, each opening its top set from each workout and the records held on it (`PersonExerciseScreen`, `GET /people/:id/exercises/:exerciseId`), follower lists and workouts in full, read-only through `GET /workouts/:id`). Their rank shows to anyone who can open the profile unless they hide it (`users.show_rank`, Settings > Account & Security > Privacy), likes and comments on finished workouts (`workout_likes`, `workout_comments` -- API-only, no client policies; seen and added to by the owner and their accepted followers, never on a trainer-logged workout; a comment deleted by its writer or the workout's owner, reportable), finding friends from contacts -- Find People's Suggested, everyone in the person's contacts on Progresso, those they don't follow yet first, read with expo-contacts' `Contact` class (since SDK 58 the old `getContactsAsync` and the rest imported from `expo-contacts` only throw -- builds up to 43 shipped it and the feature never worked; Jest's stand-in throws on them too), and a way to Full Access when iPhone was told to show only some contacts (emails and phone numbers SHA-256-hashed on the phone -- a number in E.164 form, one without its +code read as from the person's own country, `lib/phoneNumbers.ts` -- matched by `find_users_by_email_hashes` and `find_users_by_phone_hashes`, nothing stored; `users.findable_by_email` and `users.findable_by_phone`, each on unless turned off). Every account has a phone number (`users.phone_e164`, explicitly requested): asked for at sign-up, and once -- a screen that can't be skipped, `AddPhoneNumberScreen` -- of anyone who signed up before; checked as a real number for its country and never confirmed by text (a text code needs approval and an SMS provider), not unique, and never shown to anyone else -- Find People's Your Friends lists everyone who follows you or whom you follow and social push (above). What a follower can see is exactly what the Privacy Policy's "Other people on Progresso" says -- change them together. Leaderboards, public (non-follower) profiles and anything else beyond that still need approval. A block must be honoured by every way one person can reach another (search, suggestions, follows, group invites, trainer link requests): a new path that connects two people checks `loadBlocks` too. A trainer's tracked client and a group's guest are stand-in accounts no one signs in to, not people on Progresso: search and suggestions list only people who finished signing up (`users.onboarding_completed_at`), and a follow request to anyone else is refused as if they didn't exist. Account deletion (`DELETE /users/me`, `delete_account`) must keep other people's history whole; a new table that references another user's rows needs handling there. A **profile montage** is real too (explicitly requested -- `20261205100000_profile_montage.sql`, `apps/mobile/src/profile/montage`, `ProfileMontageScreen`): up to four photos or videos in any mix (videos up to a minute, made 720p MP4 by the picker; 50 MB a file), shown under the profile's buttons as one strip of four slanted panels moving slowly left to right on a seamless loop -- no title, count or controls (DESIGN.md) -- from one item up (fewer than four repeat to fill it), and not shown at all while empty. Tapped, it plays **full screen** (`montage/MontageViewer.tsx`) for anyone who can see it: each in turn, whole, a photo for 5 s and a video to its end (muted until Sound is tapped), round again from the first; tap left or right to step, hold or Pause to pause, swipe down or Close to leave. **A photo can be framed** (`20261206100000_profile_montage_framing.sql`, `montage/montageFrame.ts`, `ProfileMontageFrameScreen`): its owner drags and pinches it within its panel from the Montage page, kept as `frame_x`/`frame_y` (the photo's point at the panel's middle, 0-1) and `frame_zoom` (1-4) -- all three or none, none being the automatic framing, photos only -- never as a new file; the API hands it out with the montage, and the strip draws the photo there. It's added to, framed and changed only from Edit Profile (Profile & Personal Information > Profile Montage), never from the profile itself. Every upload goes as the file's bytes with its type (`lib/photoUpload.ts`'s `localFileBytes`), never a Blob: Storage types a Blob's form part by the Blob alone, and one read from a file on the phone has none, so it arrived as text/plain and the montage's bucket (JPEG, MP4 and QuickTime only) refused it. And a photo that changes always goes to a new file, never over the old one (`uniquePhotoPath`): the public buckets are served through Supabase's CDN, which can go on handing out what it cached at an address after the file there is replaced, so other people would see the old picture -- a profile picture included (`lib/avatarUpload.ts`; pictures from before were all `<user>/avatar.jpg`), its old file deleted only once the profile points at the new one (or at none). Everything else about a person -- name, username, bio, picture, montage and its framing -- lives only on their own rows and is read fresh by the API on every request, never copied anywhere, and every profile page reads it again each time it comes into view. Videos play muted on a loop (`expo-video`, one player per video shared by its panels); it pauses while its page isn't in view or the app is in the background, and holds still under Reduce Motion. Seen by its owner and their accepted followers -- exactly who sees their workouts -- through the profile API (`PeopleService.montage`: a private `profile-montage` bucket, hour-long signed links; a montage that can't be read never stops a profile opening), never across a block. Only the owner adds, reorders (`reorder_profile_montage`), frames and removes, straight from the app under own-row RLS; the database holds four at most at positions 0-3 without gaps, only the order and a photo's framing can change once added, and a row can only point at a file in its owner's folder (the API signs whatever a row points at). Removing one deletes its row, then its file; account deletion empties their folder with their photos. The Privacy Policy says who sees it -- change them together. Built and tested for iPhone (Android is on hold). Exercise libraries are shared too (explicitly requested -- `apps/api/src/exercises/shared-exercises.service.ts`): a trainer and their active clients see each other's custom exercises, and a person sees the people they follow's in a Friends tab -- never a tracked client's, and never across a block. A friend's exercise is used as it is, never copied (`20261124100000_friend_exercises_used_as_they_are.sql`): the workout, split day or rep range points at it, only its creator changes it, and the change reaches everyone using it; it's readable to someone who follows its creator (not across a block) or has logged or planned it (`user_can_use_exercise`), and deleting the creator's account moves everyone using it onto a copy of their own. Sets, records and top sets stay keyed to the person, never to whose exercise it is. Leaderboards and anything else beyond that scope still require explicit approval before being built.

Micronutrients (explicitly requested -- `supabase/nutrients`, `20261214100000_micronutrients.sql`, `apps/mobile/src/nutrition/micronutrients`, `MicronutrientsScreen`, `NutrientDetailScreen`) are real too: 25 vitamins and minerals, fibre, and saturated fat, trans fat and cholesterol, from the foods logged, against Health Canada's Dietary Reference Intakes. **Every reference value comes from Health Canada's tables as published** -- `supabase/nutrients/fetch_dri.py` downloads the three DRI pages into `sources/health-canada-dri.json` (cells and footnotes word for word, each page's "date modified"), and `npm run db:nutrients` (`generate.mjs`) reads each nutrient's column (named in `nutrients.mjs`), checks its unit, every life stage and a few published values, and writes the app's `nutrientCatalog.generated.ts`, the API's `micronutrient-fields.generated.ts` and the seed migration (`nutrient_definitions`, `nutrient_reference_values`) -- never edit those, or type a value, by hand. An RDA and an AI are told apart (the table's asterisk); a UL is shown apart from the recommendation, never as a goal, and where Health Canada set none ("ND") none is shown, with its note; a UL that covers supplements or one form only (`ulOnlySupplements`: vitamins A and E, niacin, folate, magnesium -- the generator refuses it without Health Canada's own note saying so) is never compared with a food log. Saturated fat, trans fat and cholesterol have no number, only Health Canada's guidance ("As low as possible..."). Values are chosen in one place (`referenceValues.ts`): by age (the profile's birthday), the tables' sex categories (the profile's gender, or chosen on the page when it's neither) and pregnancy or breastfeeding (female, 14-50) -- never weight or activity; the last two choices are kept on the phone per person (`referenceSettings.ts`), never sent. With what's needed missing, no value is chosen. **What each nutrient page says is a plain rewording of a source read in full** -- MedlinePlus articles, Health Canada's notes -- each listed and linked; NIH's fact sheets and the National Academies' reports refused automated reading, so they aren't cited; a section with no readable source (manganese, dietary cholesterol) says so rather than saying anything. **Amounts:** a food's are `foods.micronutrients` (per serving), a log's `food_logs.micronutrients` (for the amount logged, snapshotted like its other figures, scaled when its quantity changes, copied with it); each is nutrient id to amount in the nutrient's unit, and **a nutrient the data doesn't give is absent, never 0** -- a day's total is what the foods with data add up to, and every screen says how many foods it's from; a week's average is over the days with data for it, never a day with nothing logged. They come from USDA FoodData Central (the importer keeps `usda_food_reference.micronutrients_per_100g`; a search caches them onto the shared food; `fill_usda_micronutrients()` gives them to cached foods and people's unchanged copies after an import -- never a log) and Open Food Facts' label data (a 0 counts only for what a label must declare; vitamins A, D, E and folate aren't read, their label units can't be trusted); a recipe has one only where every ingredient does; editing a food's serving or figures clears its; Progresso's own built-in foods have USDA's where their figures are an SR Legacy food's (`20261215100000_builtin_food_micronutrients.sql`: 40 of the 51, matched by FDC id at their serving's weight, calories within 2% and protein and fat within 5%; the rest have none rather than a near match); AI estimates and Quick Macro Entry have none. Logs from before this have none, and are never filled in. **The week is tracked** (`micronutrients/weekTracker.ts`, `WeekBars`): the page's This Week shows each day, Sunday to Saturday, as how many nutrients with a daily reference value reached it (the fats with guidance aren't counted; a day with nothing logged is missing, never 0; a day ahead isn't counted), and a nutrient's page shows each day's share of its target. Each topic leads with a `TopicHeader` and shows its text in full -- Health Canada's notes too, nothing behind a More (Harbir's call). **A food's vitamins and minerals show wherever it's found and logged** (`nutrition/FoodNutrients.tsx`): Search Food's page and the log step after Search, Scan Barcode or the Food Library, for the amount being logged, against the person's targets; a found food's are read by its id from `foods.micronutrients` (the API's search and barcode answers don't carry them), once per food a session. Supplements, the Canadian Nutrient File and estimating a missing amount need approval.

## Design System

[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) is the visual/design source of truth for Progresso. Read it before making any UI/UX change. Follow its documented colors, typography, spacing, radii, components, navigation, states, and accessibility rules exactly — do not invent colors, typography, spacing, radii, or component styling that conflicts with it. Do not introduce a new visual pattern that conflicts with DESIGN.md without first explaining the conflict and getting explicit approval.

## Working Process (required for every non-trivial change)

1. Inspect the existing project state before proposing changes.
2. Explain the plan: what will change and why.
3. Identify the files that will be created/modified.
4. Implement the change.
5. Run tests, type checking, and linting.
6. Review the implementation critically.
7. Fix issues found.
8. Commit meaningful milestones to Git with clear messages.

## Hard Rules

- **Work incrementally.** Do not build ahead of the current phase.
- **No unrelated changes.** Don't refactor or "clean up" code outside the scope of the task.
- **No unnecessary dependencies.** Justify any new package before adding it.
- **Don't rewrite working code** unless the task requires it.
- **Never hide errors or suppress warnings** just to make builds/tests pass.
- **Never commit secrets.** All credentials/keys live in environment variables, excluded via `.gitignore`.
- **Never modify the database schema outside of version-controlled migrations.**
- **Never make major architectural decisions silently.** If the architecture needs to change, explain the reasoning and tradeoffs first and get approval.
- **Ask when a requirement is ambiguous** rather than inventing a product decision.
- User data isolation (workouts, sets, nutrition, goals, custom exercises/foods, performance data) must be enforced at the database/security level (RLS + backend authorization), never only hidden in the UI.

## Architecture Summary (see master spec for full detail)

- **Mobile:** React Native + Expo + TypeScript
- **Backend:** Node.js + TypeScript, NestJS on the Fastify adapter
- **Database:** PostgreSQL via Supabase, version-controlled migrations, RLS, triggers where appropriate
- **Auth:** Supabase Auth (email/password, Google, Apple, MFA) -- the app offers email only for now: Google and Apple are switched off in one place (`apps/mobile/src/auth/socialSignIn.ts`), their code kept
- **Storage:** Supabase Storage
- **Subscriptions:** RevenueCat is the authoritative source of entitlement; our subscription table is a projection/cache only. Separately, each person picks a **membership** (`users.membership_tier`: `workouts`, `workouts_nutrition`, `everything`) -- free, in onboarding and Settings > Membership, changeable any time -- which only decides which parts of the app show (`membership/`): Nutrition from the second up, Trainer Mode only in Everything. It's a choice, not a purchase, so it's gated in the app; nothing logged is deleted by switching
- **Admin dashboard:** React web app
- **Mobile builds:** Expo EAS
- **Backend pattern:** Hybrid — simple RLS-protected reads/writes may go direct to Supabase; anything needing server-side business logic, cross-user-trusted computation, or a third-party integration (exercise/equipment-profile writes, food search, barcode lookup and AI food search with their shared-catalog caching, follows and the Friends feed, trainer and group sessions, push tokens, sending feedback and the admins' feedback inbox, admin ops, RevenueCat webhooks, account export/deletion) goes through the NestJS API. Workout/set CRUD, nutrition logging and the user's own custom foods, saved meals and recipes are direct-to-Supabase from the client, own-row RLS-protected — this is intentional, not a gap: PR/1RM recomputation is enforced entirely by a security-definer Postgres trigger chain (`recompute_prs_for_exercise`, see `supabase/migrations/20260823100008_pr_infrastructure.sql`) with client write access to `rep_prs`/`one_rep_maxes` revoked, so correctness holds regardless of which client path wrote the set. Nutrition log totals are computed client-side at log time and are not server-verified against the live food row — a deliberate simplicity tradeoff, not an oversight.

## Caching & Data Freshness (mobile)

No caching library is used or needed — every rule below is built on plain React state/Context plus two small shared primitives (`useSignedInResource`, the exercise cache). Do not add a caching library without explaining why these can't meet the requirement first.

- **Fetch-once-per-sign-in resources** (profile, nutrition goals, today's food log, and the signed-in user's whole workout/set/PR history) go through `useSignedInResource` (`src/lib/useSignedInResource.ts`): fetched once when the user signs in, reset on sign-out, never re-fetched on focus/navigation. A write path (`updateProfile`, `saveGoals`, `logFoodEntry`, `completeWorkout`, ...) updates the shared cache directly from its own save's response (or calls `refetch()`), so every other screen already mounted sees the change immediately with no second round trip.
- **Exercise library/picker data** uses its own per-user in-memory cache (`src/exercises/exerciseQueries.ts`), not time-based — built-ins never change, and a custom exercise only changes through `ExerciseFormScreen`, which calls `invalidateExerciseCache(userId)` after every create/update/delete. The cache only ever shows the built-ins and the person's own; **exercises shared with them** (`exercises/useSharedExercises.ts`, `GET /exercises/shared` -- a trainer's and their active clients' custom exercises, both ways, in All; the people they follow's in a Friends tab) change elsewhere, so they're read each time a library or picker opens, never cached. Using a trainer's or client's first makes it theirs (`POST /exercises/:id/copy`: their own of the very same machine -- name, gym, grip and photo -- or a copy with its photo, gym and grip), so what they log never depends on the link lasting; a friend's is used as it is (above). A live workout's picker (and a group workout's) opens on a tab of its own, **Last Time** -- its **previous exercises** (`workouts/previousExercises.ts`, `usePreviousExercises`) -- beside **All Exercises**, the whole library (it opens on All Exercises for someone with no history yet): one database call, `previous_exercises_by_muscle_group` (security invoker, so RLS decides -- the person's own finished workouts, a trainer's logging for them included), and the split day's muscle groups, both read once as the workout opens (the finished workouts they come from don't change while it's going) -- never the whole history. Each suggestion is the exercise by its own id from the library the picker already has; one no longer in it is never stood in for by a look-alike.
- **Feed's own items** (the signed-in user's own workouts) and **the live-workout/group/session check** are never staleness-gated — they're refetched on every focus, because they're exactly what a one-hop edit-and-return (finish a workout, edit a past workout) changes, and a live session must always be current.
- **Feed's friends section, its notification/goal badges, and its next-workout preview** — things that only change from elsewhere (someone else's activity, a notification acted on minutes ago, a split edited) — use a 45s staleness window (`src/lib/focusFreshness.ts`) before refocusing refetches them, so switching tabs quickly doesn't re-fire these needlessly.
- **Workout History's and Profile's own recent-workout lists** always refetch on focus, for the same one-hop-mutation reason as Feed's own items -- and so does Workout History's Next Workout (at the top of its calendar widget), which finishing a workout one hop away moves on (Feed's, which only changes from elsewhere, keeps its 45s gate). Both work the next day out the same way (`computeNextWorkout`), and both Start Workouts open the Start Workout page (`NewWorkout`), which starts the day (`startPlannedWorkout`).
- **The week's figures** -- the Nutrition screen's weekly calorie budget (the week of the day shown, through that day: the day's own foods are the ones already on the page, so the bar moves on every log; the week's read is made again each time the day changes) and Progress's Weekly Summary -- are read each time their page opens, with no staleness gate: a section page opens fresh, and what they show changes one hop away.
- **Any other day on the Nutrition screen** -- before today, or ahead of it to plan -- (`src/nutrition/useDayLogs.ts`) is read when it's moved to and again on every focus, with no staleness gate -- a food logged to it from the logging screens (`{ day }` in their route params, logged at that day's date via `loggedAtFor`) lands there one hop away. Only the latest read is kept, so swiping quickly never shows one day's foods under another's date. Only today's logs live in the shared food-log cache; another day's log is never added to it. Every day read is remembered for the session (`lastShown`, below), so a day swiped back to shows at once while it's read again, and the days either side of the one shown are read ahead (`readAheadDayLogs`, once per day per session) so a swipe lands on its foods -- yesterday's comes from Copy Yesterday's own read (`readDayLogs`). Food logged to a day ahead isn't counted in Progress's Weekly Summary until that day comes (its food read stops at the end of today). The screen shows today again whenever it's left and come back to (focus after a blur), except from a way to log, which comes back to the day it logged to.
- **Micronutrients** (`MicronutrientsScreen`, `NutrientDetailScreen`) read the day's foods exactly as the Nutrition screen does -- today's from the shared food log, another day's for itself on every focus (`useDayLogs`) -- and the week's (`useWeekLogs`, Sunday to Saturday up to today) for the week's tracker and Week, as the page opens and on every focus, no staleness gate: a food logged or changed one hop away is in them. The reference values and the explanations are in the app (generated), never fetched; the choices that pick them are read from the phone as the page opens.
- **Nutrition shortcuts** -- Today's Copy Yesterday list and the Food Library's Saved Meals, Usual and Recent -- are read each time their page opens (a section page opens fresh) and again after a save on that page; no staleness gate, since a meal or recipe edited one hop away must show at once.
- **The Trainer Workspace's Activity and Sessions tabs** always refetch on focus too: the trainer's own logging and live sessions (one hop away, under Sessions) change both, and a live session must always be current.
- **The app theme** (Settings > Appearance, -- `src/theme/ThemeChoices.tsx`; onboarding has no theme step for now, so everyone starts on the default; `src/theme/AppThemeProvider.tsx`) is kept on the phone, not the account, and read once before the first screen shows (`AppShell` waits for it). Components read it through `useAppTheme` (`src/theme/appThemeContext.ts`, which has no profile dependency); a shared component's default colour is the theme's accent, never the static `colors.accent`.
- **Achievements** (`achievements/AchievementsProvider.tsx`): the catalogue, your awards and your figures are read once per sign-in and reset on sign-out; the server is asked to award (`award_my_achievements` -- never a client-side count) as you sign in, when the app comes back to the front, when Achievements opens, and 1.5 s after the shared workout, record or food caches change. After each run your awards and progress are read again, so awards the server made meanwhile (overnight, as someone opened your profile, at a claim) come in too; every award newer than the last you were shown (`users.achievements_seen_at`) is queued for `AchievementUnlockHost`, which holds it while a live workout (solo, group or a trainer's session) or the Share page is open -- a finished workout's unlocks show once its Share page is left (Harbir's call), and marked seen on the server once shown. Claiming a tracked history in Trainer Access asks for an award run straight away.
- **A profile montage** comes with the profile (read each time it opens, like the social pages below), its links signed for an hour; the Montage page reads your own each time it opens, and your profile shows a change the next time it's in view. Links change on every read, so nothing is cached by its link: a photo is kept by expo-image under its item's id, and a video on the phone once fetched (`profile/montage/montageMedia.ts`) -- an item never changes, so neither goes stale -- and a video's player follows the item, never its link, so a re-read never restarts it.
- **Social pages** -- a person's profile and workouts, an exercise of theirs, follower and following lists, a workout's likes and comments, and each feed card's like and comment counts (`social/useSocialSummaries.ts`) -- are read each time they open (and the counts whenever the cards change), with no staleness gate and no cache: they change elsewhere (someone else follows, likes, comments). A like shows at once and is undone if it doesn't save.
- **Feedback & Suggestions' list and the admins' Feedback Inbox** are read on every focus, with no staleness gate: feedback just sent from the form, or a status just changed on a review, is one hop away. One piece of feedback, and an admin's review of one, are read when opened.
- **Reminders** (`notifications/RemindersProvider.tsx`) never fetch for themselves except the next split day (`fetchNextWorkout`, two queries) -- read as the person signs in, when a workout is finished or the split changes, and on coming back to the front; everything else in the plan comes from the shared caches already loaded, and it's made again (a moment after the last change) whenever they change.
- **Progress's Cardio tab** reads the person's whole cardio history (`fetchCardioHistory`, paged) each time it opens, with no staleness gate -- a workout finished or edited one hop away changes it. The last time each activity was done (`useRecentCardio`, `fetchRecentCardio`: the person's finished workouts, not the one going) -- what Add Cardio offers first and the live workout's Cardio page shows as Last Time and Do It Again -- is read once as the page opens (the live workout's, Log/Edit Past Workout's).
- **Today's steps** (Feed's Steps card) are read from Apple Health on every Feed focus and whenever the app comes back to the front, with no staleness gate: it's a read on the phone, not a network request. **Progress's Steps tab** (`health/useStepsHistory.ts`) reads the last four weeks' daily counts the same way, each time it opens and whenever the app comes back to the front. A trainer's view of a client has no Steps tab: steps are only ever the phone's own.
- **The home-screen widgets** never fetch for themselves; the app hands them what it already has (`src/widgets/widgetUpdates.ts`), one update at a time in the order asked: Today's Nutrition from the shared food log and daily targets on every change (`WidgetSync`), Next Workout from Feed's own hero (handed over again at once on every Feed focus, from what Feed already knows) and from the live workout or group as it opens, Today's Steps from Feed's read (or the Steps tab's). The one read made for a widget is `refreshNextWorkout` (two queries) when a workout or group ends, so Next Workout moves on without waiting for Feed. A widget shows what it was last handed; Today's Nutrition and Today's Steps turn over at midnight on their own, and every widget's date does. A change of theme (or the first launch after one) reads what each widget has and redoes it in the new look, leaving one already in it alone.
- **Pages show all at once** (`src/lib/useShowAllAtOnce.ts`): the first time a page opens, its placeholders stay until every part it reads is in -- Feed's own and friends' workouts, live check, next workout (a failed read counts as in), today's steps and the cards' likes; a profile (anyone's, and yours on You) its workouts' likes, and on You your own figures; Workout History's next workout, month and recent list; Nutrition Today's week; Progress Overview's Weekly Summary -- or three seconds pass, so one slow part never holds the rest back for long. It only decides when the page first shows; every read still happens exactly as the rules here say.
- **What a section page last showed** (`src/lib/lastShown.ts`) -- Feed's first page, friends section, today's steps, the cards' like and comment counts, next-workout preview and open workouts (the live workout itself notes it as it opens and clears it when finished or cancelled -- `feed/liveWorkoutMemory.ts` -- so Feed never shows "start your next workout" while one is going), Workout History's month, recent list and next workout, each other day on the Nutrition screen, the You tab's profile (your profile, how many follow requests are waiting, first workouts and their likes), the Trainer Workspace's Activity feed and Sessions page, Progress's Weekly Summary (only for the same week) -- is remembered in memory for the session, per user, and forgotten on sign-out. A section page is opened fresh on every section switch (`navigation/sectionNavigation.ts`), so it starts from that instead of a blank page and a spinner, then refetches exactly as the rules above say. It never replaces a fetch and is never the source of truth; don't read it anywhere but a page's own initial state.
- Do not add a staleness gate to a screen's own just-mutated data, and do not remove one from data that only ever changes elsewhere, without re-checking which case it is first.

## Key Domain Concepts (do not conflate these)

- **Top set:** heaviest weight completed for an exercise within one specific workout. It's never stored: editing one (`progress/EditTopSetSheet.tsx`, for a top set logged wrong -- from Progress's Top Sets, an exercise's own page, or a trainer's view of an active client) changes the set itself in that workout, so the PR triggers and every figure follow; a trainer's edit goes through their RLS and is recorded (`set.edited`) like any other.
- **Rep-count PR:** heaviest weight ever logged for an exercise at a specific rep count.
- **True 1RM:** only ever comes from a logged set of exactly 1 rep. Never estimated from higher-rep sets. Lives in a dedicated "Maxes" section.
- **Progression cue:** a suggestion, never a record. Each person has one rep range (`users.rep_range_min`/`rep_range_max`). When a set reaches the top of it, the next time that exercise is done its slide in Last Time's Sets says to go up ("Hit 10! Go up in weight today." -- never by how much); short of the top nothing is said -- never "bad" or "go lighter". Worked out in the app from logged sets (`workouts/progression.ts`); it never changes a weight, a PR or a Top Set.
- **Which rep range applies:** an exercise's own range -- an "exception" (`exercise_progression_settings`, set from its menu in the live workout or Settings > Workout Preferences > Rep Range Exceptions) -- wins over the person's (`users.rep_range_min/max`); a range may be one number. A trainer sets an active client's range and exceptions through the API (`/trainer/clients/:id/rep-range(s)`, link-checked and audited) -- the same rows, so the client sees and can change them; there is no separate trainer layer. There's no weight step: an exception is a rep range only, and a step saved before steps went (`exercise_progression_settings.weight_increment_kg`) is ignored, and cleared when that exercise's range is next saved.
- **Daily targets:** the daily targets (`nutrition_goals`) are only ever changed by the person saving them. Recommended: protein 0.8 g and fat 0.3 g per lb of body weight, to the pound as it's shown (`macroGuidance`), carbs (calories - 4 x protein - 9 x fat) / 4 (`carbsFor`), each rounded down to the gram -- calories and macros are shown as whole numbers rounded down everywhere (`nutrition/wholeFigures.ts`), so a recommendation is saved exactly as shown. Every target is still the person's to type; on Nutrition Goals a blank macro field is its recommendation (shown faded, saved as shown). A calorie estimate is applied only on a tap, nothing is saved until the person saves, and a weight change never rewrites a saved target.
- **Calorie goal (cut, maintain, bulk):** never stored -- it's read from the daily calorie target against the maintenance estimate Nutrition Goals' calculator works out from the profile (`calorieGoalFor`, `nutrition/nutritionCalculations.ts`): 150 cal or more below is a cut, 150 or more above a bulk, anything nearer maintaining (half the calculator's 300 step). The weekly budget reads the week for it: under the target is on track for a cut, at or over it on track for a bulk. No profile details, no goal shown.
- **Fitness goals:** up to three (`users.fitness_goals`, in the order picked); `fitness_goal` is kept as the first of them by a trigger, for app versions that read or send one.
- **Claiming tracked history:** a client who enters the code their trainer gave them -- in Settings > Trainer Code or Trainer Access, or while signing up (onboarding's trainer-code step, claimed right after the account is created) -- gets that history as their own (`claim_placeholder_history`), and the trainer stays their trainer, active. **Only workout data moves** (Harbir's rule, `20261125100000_claim_moves_only_workouts.sql`): the workouts, with their exercises and sets, and the records rebuilt from them. Nothing else the trainer entered for the tracked client -- name, birthday, height, weight, the rep ranges they set -- is copied, not even into a blank: the person's profile is exactly what they made it signing up. The app reads the history, records and exercises again after a claim (they're read once per sign-in, and at sign-up before the claim).
- **Split day plan:** a day's planned exercises (`workout_split_day_exercises`) go into a workout as the day is done; the workout is its own record from then on. The next day is the one after the last completed day -- a missed workout never advances it. **A day with a plan runs as a guided workout** (explicitly requested -- `workouts/guidedWorkout.ts`): it starts on the plan's first exercise with one set, only the exercise being done is open (the others fold to a row each), the plan's next exercise waits in Up Next -- one tap adds it, or another from the plan -- and each set adds the next by itself once filled in, up to the sets planned; moving on sets aside a set left untouched. Which workouts are guided is kept on the phone (like the typed drafts) and forgotten at Finish or Cancel; a day without a plan, or a workout opened elsewhere, is the usual live workout.
- **One machine:** a custom exercise is told apart from look-alikes by its photo, gym, equipment (`equipment`: dumbbell, barbell, kettlebell, cable, machine, bodyweight or other), grip (`grip`: how it's held, or for a leg exercise where the feet go -- `exercises/gripChoices.ts`), attachment (`attachment`: what's held, the bar or handle -- grip and attachment were one field until `20261127100000_exercise_setup_details.sql`, which moved the grips out), how it's loaded (`load_type`), where its cable starts (`pulley_position`: high, mid, low, adjustable or other) and, on an exercise saved before it was retired, its old heavier/lighter `pulley` -- `exercises/machineLoad.ts`; names may repeat, even at one gym. Setup notes (`setup_details`: seat height, backrest, ...) are documentation, never part of it. Anything that matches one person's exercise to another's (a trainer logging for a client, a live session, claiming tracked history, account deletion, copying a shared one) matches on all of them with the name (`exercise_machine_key`, nine arguments), never the name alone, and every copy carries them, setup notes included. Records, top sets and history belong to the exercise they were logged against, so two setups never share them; editing the setup of an exercise with logged workouts asks first and offers to save it as a new exercise instead (`ExerciseFormScreen`). Create Exercise asks only what fits the equipment (`exercises/equipment.ts`). A machine's line (`machineLine`) names them in the Exercise Library and picker, the live workout's card and an exercise's own page; Progress's PR and top-set rows, and what friends see, show gym, grip and attachment. An older app version that sends a grip as the attachment has it saved as the grip (the API's `isLegacyGrip`). The old "how it feels" note (`feel_notes`) is no longer asked for, shown or shared.
- **The built-in library** (explicitly requested -- `supabase/catalog`, `20261128100000_builtin_exercise_library.sql`, `20261129100000_complete_exercise_library.sql` and `20261130100000_simpler_exercise_names.sql`, `apps/mobile/src/exercises/illustrations`): 396 exercises -- 97 dumbbell, 95 barbell (the trap bar, safety squat and Swiss bars and the landmine among them), 62 cable, 62 machine (selectorized, plate-loaded and Smith), 13 kettlebell, 36 bodyweight and 31 other (bands, suspension trainer, medicine ball, battle ropes, sled, plates) -- each with one canonical row; a duplicate (Reverse Fly / Rear Delt Fly, Lying Triceps Extension / Skull Crusher, ...) is one exercise with the other name as a search alias. **Each cable attachment or machine grip that changes the movement is its own exercise** (Seated Cable Row -- Close-Grip V-Handle, -- Rope Attachment, ...; a brand, a seat notch or a slightly different hand spacing is not), and a built-in records its setup like a custom exercise does (`equipment`, `grip`, `attachment`, `pulley_position`, `load_type`), so `machineLine` names it. Machines and cables were left out of the built-ins from 2026-10-05 (`20261005100000`: they differ gym to gym, so each person adds their own, with its gym and photo); Harbir's complete-library brief brought them back -- the 18 hidden machine and cable built-ins are active again under their standard setup ("Lat Pulldown" -> "Lat Pulldown — Wide-Grip Straight Bar", "Leg Press" back as it was, same row, the old name an alias), and a person's own per-gym machines are still the way to keep two gyms' records apart. Once the library was complete, every custom exercise with an identical built-in was moved onto it and deleted (`20261204100000_custom_exercises_to_builtins.sql`, a one-off data fix by id -- history for everyone who logged it, tracked clients included, plans, setups, exceptions, the photo as its owner's machine photo, records worked out again); one whose name marks a stack or setup, too general to place, logged differently, or with no match stayed custom. An assisted machine or a band is reps only (`bodyweight`): its weight is assistance, which would read backwards as a record -- an assisted measurement would need approval. **The catalogue in `supabase/catalog/*.mjs` is the source of truth**: `npm run db:catalog` (`supabase/catalog/generate.mjs`) checks it and writes the migration and the app's `builtinCatalog.generated.ts` -- never edit either by hand; a new built-in or a changed guide is a catalogue change and a new migration (point `MIGRATION` in `generate.mjs` at a new file once the last is applied; the applied `20261128100000` is never written again). The migration updates every catalogue row, so running it again changes nothing. Each built-in has a `slug` (its illustration's key -- only built-ins have one), `secondary_muscle_groups` (worked out from the muscles its illustration highlights, so the two never disagree), `movement_pattern`, a guide (`instructions`, `setup_steps`, `execution_steps`, `common_mistakes`), `search_aliases` and an optional `illustration_url` (a hosted image that would replace the drawing without an app update). **Names are simple wherever the drawing already shows the detail** (Harbir's rule): "Leg Press", not "45-Degree Leg Press"; "Pec Deck", not "Pec Deck Machine"; "Dumbbell Curl", not "Standing Dumbbell Bicep Curl"; a cable exercise that only comes one way without its handle ("Cable Face Pull") -- a handle stays in the name only where the same movement comes with several (the seated cable rows, pulldowns, pushdowns and cable curls), and a word stays where dropping it would leave two exercises one name (Hip Thrust Machine, Ab Crunch Machine). A migration finds a built-in by its slug to rename it (`20261130100000_simpler_exercise_names.sql`), the longer name kept as an alias where it was ever in use. The migrations only rename a built-in that was already here and never changes an existing one's muscle group, measurement or sides, so history, records and achievements read exactly as before. **The illustrations are drawn in the app, not images**, in the "clean 3D" style Harbir chose (Style 2, matched to his nine-exercise reference): one illustrated instructor in a black tank top and shorts, with a face (`illustrations/rig.ts` -- a simple 3D figure whose hands and feet are placed and the elbows and knees solved), each hand a closed fist with four fingers and a thumb around its grip (`fistPiece`), the equipment (`props.ts`; the gym beyond free weights -- cable columns, every attachment, machine seats, pads, stacks and levers, the Smith machine, kettlebells, bands, straps -- in `gym.ts`; machines are built the way the real ones are: a weight-stack tower with its plates, guide rods and header, every lever's pivot carried on a post to the floor, seats on a carriage and foot, the 45-degree leg press's sled riding rails under it with plates on its horns), projected, depth-sorted and softly shaded into SVG shapes with gradients (`render.ts`, `shading.ts`); the instructor is plain, and the worked muscles are coloured on a grey anatomy figure beside the drawing -- head to toe, every muscle marked out, from the front and from behind side by side (`muscleMap.ts`; `muscles.ts`: primary red, secondary yellow) -- with a key naming each muscle on a line of its own ("Chest (Upper)", "Back (Lats)"); each position is named in a pill, the first Start and the last what the movement does ("Press", "Hinge", "Lift" -- `actionLabels.ts`, by movement pattern, which the generated catalogue carries, and by exercise where its frames say otherwise: the catalogue's `action`); each exercise's poses are in `illustrations/art/*.ts`. **How to Do It shows every built-in in exactly three phases** (`guidePhases`, Harbir's reference design): the setup, the movement and the way back -- drawn from the first position again and named for it (`returnLabel`: Lower, Stand, Return, ...) -- or each position of one drawn in three (the push press, the get-ups); the holds are drawn from their setup (knees down, hips down, lying flat) into the hold. Each phase has a numbered row of text and the page has three Key Tips: `supabase/catalog/howto.mjs` sorts each exercise's own `steps` into its phases (`PHASES`, a role per step -- never guessed at build time) and picks its tips from a shared library of cues (`TIPS`, `EXERCISE_TIPS`), and `npm run db:catalog` checks every active built-in has all three rows and three different tips and writes `illustrations/builtinGuides.generated.ts` -- app-only, the database's guide is unchanged. **Swiped left, a second page: Technique & Setup** (explicitly requested): what makes this exercise, or this variation, what it is -- its grip (palms diagrams: overhand, underhand, neutral, angled, and the hands' width), the actual cable attachment, where the feet go (on a plate, or the stance from above), the bench's angle, and close looks at the exercise's own picture -- its 3D render, else its drawing (`TechniqueVisuals.tsx`, cropped around the hands, feet, knee, hips, torso or head from the positions the renderer records -- `anchors` -- with a straight-back line, a knee-angle arc or a plumb line in the accent). Each card is a short title and a line or two in plain words; only what applies is shown (one to three cards, never two with the same picture). The rules and wording are `supabase/catalog/technique.mjs` (`techniqueFor`, with each exercise's own cards in `OWN`), checked and written into the same generated file. Page dots and a chevron either side say which page is showing. **Rendered 3D phases** (explicitly requested): `tools/exercise-renders` is a Blender pipeline -- a rigged realistic human (Microsoft Rocketbox, MIT) posed from the app's own solved poses, the equipment rebuilt from the app's own solids in the general look of gym equipment, no brand's (Harbir's call; `recordSolids` in `props.ts` hands them over), the hands and what they hold kept clear of the body, one studio and camera -- whose WebPs (bundled, about 33 MB: `apps/mobile/assets/exercise-renders`, mapped by the generated `illustrations/renders.generated.ts` with each frame's body positions) replace the drawing in How to Do It (its three phases still, side by side, in `ExercisePhases` -- nothing steps or cycles by itself), in list thumbnails (cropped to the body) and in Technique & Setup's close looks. Every active built-in is rendered; one without a render (a new built-in, until it's rendered) keeps its drawing, and a changed pose or piece of equipment means rendering that exercise again. Its README lists the asset sources and licences; buying assets needs approval. **An exercise's own guide cards** (explicitly requested -- `tools/guide-cards`): Harbir's generated cards, two an exercise -- page 1 the demonstration and instructions, page 2 Technique & Setup -- each one picture with its words in it, bundled as WebPs (`apps/mobile/assets/guide-cards`, mapped by the generated `illustrations/guideCards.generated.ts`). Where an exercise has them, How to Do It is just those two pages, swiped between -- each the page's full width at its own shape, from the top, never shrunk or cropped (scrolled if taller than the room), pinched to zoom, the dots right under them; the sheet's own title gives way to the card's -- shown at once from the exercise's extras, with nothing to read; VoiceOver reads the guide's own words (the card's are in its picture). The rest keep the drawn page. `cards.json` says which sheet column is which slug (a sheet: a row of exercises, page 1 over page 2 -- cut in the gap between cards and each trimmed to its own border, or on the lines of a sheet put together from screenshots), a `<slug>_1.png` or `_2.png` made on its own takes its page's place, and `publish.py` checks each is an active built-in, listed once; the sources stay outside the repo. In a live workout each built-in has a **How to Do It** pill under its name (`exercises/HowToPill.tsx`) that opens it. **A photo of your own machine for a built-in** (explicitly requested -- `exercise_user_photos`, `20261203100000_builtin_exercise_photos.sql`; `exercises/ownPhotos.ts`, plugged in by `ownPhotoQueries.ts`): added from the exercise's page (the library opens a built-in on its page, `ProgressExerciseDetail`, where How to Do It stays a tap away) or the live workout's exercise menu, kept in the person's own equipment-photos folder, one per person per built-in (own-row RLS, built-ins only by trigger), read once a sign-in and forgotten at sign-out. It replaces the drawing only where that person's own `ExercisePhoto` is given the exercise's id -- the library, the picker, the exercise's page and the live workout -- never on anyone else's workout or profile. Tests check every catalogue exercise has one, every hand and foot reaches, its primary muscles show on its anatomy figure, and no two exercises draw the same. A muscle filter (the Exercise Library's and the picker's) lists only the exercises whose primary muscle group it is -- never one that only works it secondarily (Harbir's call; an Also Works section was dropped); achievements and Weekly Summary count only an exercise's own muscle group too. The list's extras are read in a query of their own (`exercises/builtinExtras.ts`, plugged in by `exerciseGuide.ts`), so a library or workout never waits on them or breaks without them. Carries and static holds are `timed` (the weight isn't recorded -- a weight-and-time measurement would need approval).
- **Measurement type:** what an exercise's sets record -- `weight_reps`, `bodyweight` (reps only) or `timed` (a duration). A weighted variant (Weighted Pull-Up) is its own `weight_reps` exercise, never a flag on a bodyweight one. Each logged exercise snapshots its type (`workout_exercises.measurement_type`, set by trigger), so changing an exercise's type never rewrites history. Top sets, rep PRs, 1RMs, volume and strength charts are `weight_reps` only; a bodyweight exercise's record is its most reps (`rep_records`) and a timed one's its longest (`duration_records`), both trigger-maintained like PRs. In the live workout a timed set can be timed (`workouts/setTimers.ts`): a timer is a start moment and a total, never a counter, so it stays right through the background, a locked phone or a remount; it's kept on the device for the workout like the typed drafts and is never sent anywhere -- pausing it (or Finish, for one still going) saves its time as the set's `duration_seconds`, exactly like a typed time.
- **Live sets save as they're typed** (`workouts/useSetAutosave.ts`): there's no check to tap. A set is done -- `completed_at` set, so it counts for PRs and records -- once its fields hold what its exercise records, saved a moment after the typing stops and again on every change (emptied, it reopens). A new set starts empty -- never filled in from the set before it or from last session (Last Time shows above the sets instead). Finish asks first ("Finish Workout?"), then saves what's on the screen and asks about any set left with an empty field -- fill it in, or remove it. A workout with no finished set is never saved: Finish offers to discard it instead, and the Friends feed and profiles leave out any finished with nothing in it. The same in a group workout and a trainer's live session. In a group an exercise added is added to everyone's workout, so finishing the group takes it off the workout of anyone who finished no set on it (`GroupsService.finish`, soft-deleted like Remove) -- people often do their own variation -- and each person keeps only what they did.
- **Warm-up sets** (explicitly requested -- `sets.is_warmup`, `20261202100000_warmup_sets.sql`): tapping a set's number in a live workout (`workouts/SetNumber.tsx`) marks it a warm-up -- a small "W" on a tint of the accent -- and tapping again makes it a working set; a unilateral set's two sides go together. A warm-up counts toward nothing (Harbir's call): it's shown with its workout ("Warm-up" on the workout's page, a little quieter) but is never a PR, 1RM, rep or duration record, top set, part of volume or a set count, a progression cue or Last Time, or part of an achievement figure (a workout of warm-ups alone isn't a valid one). The record triggers, achievement and profile functions leave it out (`and not s.is_warmup`), so do the app's counting rules (`loggedSets`, `completedSetsOnly`; `shownSets` for what a finished workout shows) and the feed's figures; a friend's workout carries the mark. Group workouts and a trainer's live session don't offer it yet. Since nothing else would say so, a one-time tip on the live workout's first exercise (`workouts/WarmupHint.tsx`) says tapping a set's number marks a warm-up; it goes once dismissed or once a warm-up is first marked, remembered on the phone. Log Past Workout and Edit Workout offer it too (tap the number), saved with the set (`createCompletedSets`, `updateSet`, and `isWarmup` to the trainer API's log).
- **Drop sets** (explicitly requested -- `sets.is_drop`, `20261207100000_drop_sets.sql`, `workouts/dropSets.ts`): a working set followed by drops -- the weight dropped and the set carried on until the person stops. **Drop Set** beside Add Set (a weighted exercise's, once a working set is filled in -- a warm-up isn't one) adds a drop under the last set filled in, below any drops already there, so they stack; a set waiting empty below (a guided workout's next) moves down one -- in the live workout through `move_sets` (security invoker: every set moved out of the way, then to its place, all at once, so `sets_live_slot_key` never trips), in Log/Edit Past Workout in the draft (Edit Workout moves saved sets with `move_sets` before saving new ones). A drop has no number of its own: it shows under its set as an arrow turning in (`SetNumber`'s `drop`), "Drop" on a finished workout's page, and belongs to the nearest working set before it in `set_index` order; removing a working set removes its drops with it. **The first set counts like any working set, records included; the drops after it** (Harbir's call) add their weight x reps to volume -- the workout's, lifetime, muscle-group and achievement volume -- but are never a set of their own in a set count, and never a PR, 1RM, rep or duration record, top set, best lift, strength gain, progression cue or Last Time. The record triggers and best/history functions leave them out (`and not s.is_drop`), `person_profile_totals` and `achievement_metrics` count their volume but not them as sets, and so do the app (`loggedSets` leaves them out, `completedSetsOnly` keeps them for volume, `workingSets` for history counts and bests) and the feed's figures. A set is never both a warm-up and a drop (`sets_warmup_or_drop`). Bodyweight and timed exercises, group workouts and a trainer's live session don't offer drops.
- **Cardio in a workout** (explicitly requested -- `workout_cardio`, `20261208100000_workout_cardio.sql`, `apps/mobile/src/cardio`): a workout holds cardio activities alongside its exercises -- before the lifting, between exercises or after it, as many as the person likes -- or nothing but cardio, always **one workout record** (never a separate workout or post for the cardio). Treadmill, Outdoor Running, Walking, Cycling, StairMaster, Elliptical, Rowing, Swimming and Other (named), each asking only what fits it -- **one definition per activity** (`cardioActivities.ts`'s `CARDIO_INFO`: its fields, first the main ones then the optional, and how its speed reads; `CARDIO_FIELDS`: how each is typed and checked), which the sheet, every page that shows cardio and Progress all follow (Harbir's cardio brief, `20261211100000_cardio_metrics.sql`): a time is required for all, and nothing else is. Treadmill: distance, speed (the number on the machine, `speed_setting`, no unit), incline. Outdoor Running and Walking: distance, avg pace (min/km). Cycling: distance, avg speed (km/h), resistance. StairMaster: level, floors, steps. Elliptical: distance, resistance, speed (the machine's). Rowing: distance, avg split (/500 m), stroke rate. Swimming: distance, pool length, laps (lengths; a blank distance is laps x pool length). Other: what it was, distance, notes. **Kilometres for everyone** -- distance in km, speed in km/h, pace per km (a split per 500 m, a swim per 100 m), whatever the weight unit; a typed pace or split is saved as the km/h it stands for (`speed_kmh`), and one left blank is worked out from the distance and time where shown -- never estimated, never stored. **No calories** anywhere in cardio: the column stays for what older app versions typed, never read, written or shown (the API hands out `calories: null` for older apps), and changing an activity keeps what its activity no longer asks for (an older rower's damper, an older treadmill's km/h) unless the activity itself changes. All typed in -- no GPS, Apple Health or other integration (that would need approval). Stored in canonical units (metres, km/h) on its own row, never a set or a workout_exercise, so PRs, records, top sets, progression, volume and set counts are exactly what they were; the row's owner is always the workout's (trigger), own-row RLS like sets, read by an active trainer, and shown to followers only through the API (a friend's workout, the Friends feed). **The live workout's cardio is a page of its own** (Harbir's call, replacing the slim line in the pinned widget): the workout swipes between **Lifting** and **Cardio** (`ActiveWorkoutScreen`'s pager), with tabs under its pinned widget saying so (`workouts/LivePageTabs.tsx` -- Cardio with how many activities it holds -- the underline following the swipe), and the first live workout on a phone nudges toward Cardio and back once (never under Reduce Motion). The Cardio page (`cardio/LiveCardioPage.tsx`) shows the cardio all together, each activity as its own widget (`CardioActivityCard`: its figures, and **what it was last time**, as an exercise's Last Time), + Add Cardio, and **Do It Again** -- the activities done lately that aren't in this workout yet, a tap opening Add Cardio on the same figures. Everything saves at once, as before. **Add Cardio** (`CardioSheet`) opens on the activities as tiles -- the ones the person did last first -- and a tap goes straight to that activity's figures, with its last time above them and **Use These** to start from them; Change goes back to the tiles keeping what's typed. Finish asks only "Finish Workout?" -- **never about cardio** (Harbir's call): for a workout with cardio and sets, Finish works the strength part's end out as its last finished set (`settleStrengthTime`: `workouts.strength_ended_at`, and each activity added after that set is `after_strength`), so the time after the last set -- a break, packing up -- is never training. **Time** (`cardio/workoutTime.ts`, the API's `feed/workout-cardio.ts` the same): strength training runs from the start to the strength part's end (or the finish) less the cardio done before it; cardio is each activity's own time; total active training is the two; the session, start to finish, is told apart from all three -- nothing stored, all worked out where shown, so an edit or removal puts every figure right. A finished workout's page shows its cardio and a Time table (Strength Training, each activity, Total Active Training, then the session); with cardio its figure is Active, not Duration; cardio alone shows its time and activities. Workout History, Feed (own and friends') and profiles show each activity on the card, Active in place of Duration, and a workout of cardio alone counts as a finished workout (the feed, Finish's empty check, achievements' valid workouts -- Workouts and Weeks Trained, never sets, volume or a muscle group, Harbir's call). Log Past Workout and Edit Workout add, change and remove cardio too (their Cardio card and the same sheet), its time within the duration typed; **Progress's Cardio tab** (`progress/CardioProgressSection.tsx`, `progress/cardioProgress.ts`, the person's own -- a trainer's view of a client has none yet) works its figures out from the cardio saved in finished workouts: a range (Week, Month, 3 Months, All Time) and an activity (All, or each one done) -- sessions, time and distance; for All each activity's share; for one, its averages (only what it recorded), all-time bests and latest sessions (each opening its workout); and trends, the last 8 weeks or 6 months as rows with bars by time, distance, sessions or pace -- no line graph, nothing from lifting; a trainer's log or edit of a client's workout has no cardio yet, nor do group workouts and a trainer's live session. **The share card** (`sharing/ShareCard.tsx`, rebuilt after Harbir's reference design: a 9:16 story in Holiday Gym's green and gold, festive through the holidays, with Share and Save to Photos) reads the saved workout (`shareCardData.ts`): Duration start to finish (its cardio in it once), Sets, Volume, Records (`isLiveRecord`, the same as its page), each exercise's top set only (`bestLoggedSet`) under a display name that's never ambiguous, and a Cardio panel with every figure each activity recorded; it measures itself and tightens (roomy, snug, tight), and only past tight says "+N more exercises"; a cardio-only workout can be shared. A finished workout's page -- yours or a friend's -- shows each activity as its own widget and the Time table after it, with **Cardio** as a chip beside the muscle tabs; feed cards give each activity its glyph. Cardio achievements are in Achievement Points (above).

- **Achievement Points (AP):** a person's lifetime AP is the sum of their `achievement_awards`, which only the server writes -- awarding a level reached, and taking back one that what's logged no longer reaches (never a social one). So AP and the rank worked out from it can go down when a workout is deleted or corrected. Never count AP or decide an award on the phone.

Weights are stored canonically in kilograms; lb/kg is a display/input preference only.

PR correctness must be maintained across set create/edit/soft-delete/restore and workout delete/restore/date-change. No stale PR records. This is enforced by DB triggers, not application code — see the Architecture Summary's backend pattern note. If a future change reassigns a `workout_exercises.exercise_id` after sets were already logged against it (no current code path does this), extend the trigger chain to cover it; the existing triggers do not fire on that column changing.

Nutrition logs must snapshot the nutrition data at the time of logging — later edits to a food database entry must not retroactively change historical logs.

- **Search Food** (`FoodsService.search`): Progresso's shared catalog, USDA FoodData Central's reference foods (`usda_food_reference`, Foundation and SR Legacy -- read only from the API) and Open Food Facts are read at the same time and ranked together (`foods/search-ranking.ts`); Open Food Facts is waited for at most 2 s (`BRANDED_SEARCH_BUDGET_MS`, what comes later is still cached), and `branded=0` leaves it out for the app's quick first answer. A USDA food is cached into the shared catalog (`provider` `usda_fdc`, `provider_food_id` its FDC id, per 100 g, verified, with USDA's citation) only when it's shown, so it has a row to log against; a query that finds nothing is spell-corrected once (`search-vocabulary.ts`, read as the server starts) and says so (`correctedQuery`). Never invent figures: a food no source has goes to AI Food Search, labelled as an estimate. In the app, a search answered once this session shows at once with nothing asked again, and typing on from a search narrows the rows on screen while the new answer comes (`nutrition/foodSearchMemory.ts`, the latest 40, forgotten at sign-out). **Scan Barcode** looks a barcode up in USDA FoodData Central's **Branded Foods** -- manufacturers' own label data, through FDC's API with an api.data.gov key (`USDA_FDC_API_KEY`, explicitly requested; with none set it's off and scans are Open Food Facts' alone) -- at the same time as Open Food Facts, and uses USDA's figures where it has the very barcode (the newest label; per 100 g scaled back to the label serving, vitamin D in IU as mcg ÷ 40; cached as `usda_fdc`, public domain, with Open Food Facts' photo credited "Photo: Open Food Facts"), else Open Food Facts'; a product cached from Open Food Facts is checked against USDA's once per server run, and USDA's row wins over Open Food Facts' for the same barcode (`foods/providers/usda-branded.provider.ts`). It answers from the cached product, but reads it from Open Food Facts again (waiting at most a second, else refreshing it in the background) when it was cached before its serving wording or micronutrients were read, or more than 30 days ago (`BARCODE_REFRESH_AFTER_DAYS`), so a label corrected there reaches the next scan; a product whose entry gives its energy only in kJ is converted (÷ 4.184), never dropped.
- **A day with nothing logged is missing, never 0 eaten:** the weekly budget names such a day, doesn't read the week for the goal while one is missing, and offers an estimate for it only on a tap, labelled and never mixed into the logged total (`nutrition/weekLogging.ts`). Never fill in a log the person didn't make.
- **Logged foods are kept in the Food Library:** logging a food that isn't the person's own (a built-in, a cached search or barcode result) first copies it into their foods once (`keepInLibrary`, matched by barcode, or by name, brand, serving and calories), and the log points at that copy -- editable and deletable like any food of theirs. A failure to copy never stops the log. Quick Macro Entry (`QuickFoodEntryScreen`) logs typed figures with no food behind them (`food_id` null, marked user-entered), and makes a food only when Save to Food Library is ticked.
- **Saved meal vs recipe (do not conflate):** a saved meal (`saved_meals`) is several foods eaten together; logging it logs **each food as its own entry**. A recipe is **one food** (a `foods` row with `recipe_servings` set, serving 1 "serving"), its figures per serving worked out client-side from `recipe_ingredients` when it's saved and stored on the food row; it logs as a single entry like any food. Editing either never changes past logs (snapshots).

## Permission Levels

- **Regular user:** own data only.
- **Support admin:** restricted administrative/support capabilities.
- **Full admin:** exercise library management, user management, support ops, analytics, subscription/billing visibility.

All administrative actions must be auditable.
